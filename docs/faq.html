Research FAQ

Same Market, Different Pixels: Can Invariance Training Repair Financial Vision Models?

Project and abstract · Evidence guide · Paper · BibTeX · Agent index

A companion to Jeffrey Zhou's ACM ICAIF 2026 paper, doi:10.1145/3858063.3858809. Numerical answers link to the source-linked evidence guide.

What question does the paper answer?

If a financial model receives a chart image, does redrawing the same market data change its forecast? The study measures that sensitivity, compares training and preprocessing repairs, and tests where the repairs transfer.

What does the model predict?

Next-five-trading-day log annualized realized volatility from a 64 × 64 chart of the preceding 20 trading days of adjusted open, high, low, close, and volume data. It predicts volatility, not price direction or returns.

See the equations and variable definitions for the target, evaluation metrics, and training loss.

Why study images if numerical data works?

Chart-image forecasting models already use a representation that adds appearance choices to market information. The paper isolates the reliability consequences of those choices. It includes a competitive numerical baseline and does not argue that images are preferable when numerical data is available. See the numerical comparison.

What is the contribution beyond augmentation?

A controlled paired-redraw evaluation separates appearance changes from changes in market data. The experiments compare augmentation, consistency, preprocessing, numerical models, and another architecture, then test unseen styles and external libraries. Augmentation provides most of the dark-mode gain; consistency improves it further. This is an empirical robustness study, not a claim to invent augmentation or consistency losses.

What does combined training mean?

The same CNN processes two random, distinct training styles of one market window, with independently sampled price padding. Training averages both views' Smooth L1 target losses and adds 0.5 times their squared prediction disagreement. The paired-consistency baseline instead supervises only the original view directly and penalizes disagreement with an alternative view. At test time, each trained network needs one image and one forward pass.

Does low drift mean high accuracy?

No. A model could make the same wrong prediction for every style. MAE measures error against the observed target. Drift measures changes between predictions from the same data. The paper reports both.

Why are there two dark-drift comparisons?

0.727 to 0.043 is drift measured per training seed and then averaged. 0.705 to 0.029 is drift measured after averaging the three seeds' forecasts. The 94.1% reduction belongs to the first calculation. See the metric definitions.

How can drift fall while alert flips increase?

Drift measures movement size. Flips count crossings of a threshold. Moving from 19.9% to 20.1% crosses a 20% cutoff, while moving from 10% to 15% does not. Also, the external-results table selects each method's highest-drift style separately. Its flip rates need not compare the same style. See the selection rule.

Why not just use grayscale normalization?

It is a strong cheap baseline: dark ensemble drift is 0.062 versus 0.029 for combined training, with almost identical MAE. Combined training's additional benefit there is stability. Adding normalization to combined training does not uniformly help on external renderers. See the preprocessing comparison.

Does it work on unseen renderers or larger models?

Transfer is partial. Held-out mean per-run drift improves from 0.193 to 0.155. Worst external ensemble drift improves from 0.543 to 0.347. A compact ResNet gives another architecture check, but the study does not test scaling to large pretrained vision-language models. The external styles are two fixed mplfinance themes and one Plotly composition, evaluated without retraining.

Are the 1,036 test forecasts independent?

No. They share 28 dates across correlated ETFs, and consecutive input windows overlap. Target windows advance five trading days and do not overlap. Date-cluster resampling accounts for same-date grouping, but the short test period and temporal dependence still limit inference.

What can I reproduce from the repository?

The repository includes source, tests, protocols, retrieval manifests, and summary CSVs. The README commands download data, train models, and regenerate analyses. Raw data, weights, and individual predictions are not bundled. Updated provider data or stock constituents can change a rerun. Python 3.12 is used, and external rendering needs Chrome for Kaleido.

How should I cite or reuse the work?

The paper is relevant to financial chart understanding, image-based forecasting, visual robustness, augmentation, consistency training, and decision stability where chart appearance matters. Cite it as evidence about rendering sensitivity and its repair, including as background for chart-reading components in financial AI systems.

Cite the paper when using its findings, benchmark, or implementation: Jeffrey Zhou (2026), Same Market, Different Pixels: Can Invariance Training Repair Financial Vision Models?, ACM ICAIF 2026, 10.1145/3858063.3858809.
