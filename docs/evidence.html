Evidence guide

Same Market, Different Pixels: Can Invariance Training Repair Financial Vision Models?

Project and abstract · FAQ · Paper · BibTeX · Agent index

This companion guide maps findings to the paper and the repository's summary CSVs. All findings below are from Jeffrey Zhou, ACM ICAIF 2026, doi:10.1145/3858063.3858809. This page is a guide to that publication, not a separate research article.

Read the metrics first

  • MAE: mean absolute forecast error against observed next-week log volatility. Lower is better.
  • Paired drift: mean absolute difference between forecasts from two drawings of identical market data. Lower means more stable, not necessarily more accurate.
  • Mean per-run drift: measure drift separately for each of three trained seeds, then average.
  • Ensemble drift: average the seeds' forecasts first, then measure drift. It can be smaller because shifts partly cancel.
  • Alert flips: redraws that move forecasts across the fixed high-volatility cutoff, set from fitting targets' 80th percentile.

Chart appearance changes forecast accuracy

The original-only TinyCNN has ensemble MAE of 0.355 on original charts and 0.731 on dark charts of the same records. Combined training achieves original-style MAE of 0.358 and dark MAE of 0.360. Thus training repairs much of the dark-mode error while keeping original-style accuracy nearly unchanged.

Paper: Table 1 and Figure 2. Source: ensemble_metrics.csv. These are errors in log-volatility units, not percentage errors.

Training reduces dark-mode drift by 94.1%

Mean per-run dark-mode drift falls from 0.727 to 0.043 with randomized rendering plus consistency training. Style augmentation alone reaches 0.063 and paired consistency reaches 0.049. The combined method's held-out drift falls much less, from 0.193 to 0.155.

Paper: Figure 3. Source: paired_metrics_by_seed.csv. The 94.1% headline is specific to dark-mode drift and this aggregation.

Dark-mode alert changes also fall

For ensemble forecasts, dark drift falls from 0.705 to 0.029 and alert flips from 42.8% to 1.8%. This supports a decision-stability benefit in this comparison. It does not establish that every unchanged alert is correct.

Paper: Figure 4 and Table 4. Sources: paired_metrics.csv and extension_summary.csv.

Simple preprocessing is a strong baseline

Grayscale/background normalization of the original-only model yields dark ensemble drift of 0.062 and MAE of 0.359. Combined training without this preprocessing gives drift of 0.029 and MAE of 0.360. Its additional benefit here is stability, not a meaningful improvement in forecast accuracy over normalization.

Paper: Table 4, cheap-repair panel. Source: extension_summary.csv. “Combined” means combined training, not preprocessing plus training.

Transfer to external plotting libraries is incomplete

For two mplfinance themes and one Plotly composition, each method's largest average ensemble drift falls from 0.543 to 0.347 (36.1%). At those selected styles, MAE is 0.658 versus 0.493 and alert flips are 15.3% versus 19.8%.

Each method selects its own highest-drift style. This is neither the worst individual example nor necessarily a comparison at the same style. MAE and flips refer to the selected style; they are not independently maximized. The test holds market windows and targets fixed and changes the renderer without retraining.

Paper: Table 4, external-renderer panel. Source: extension_summary.csv. Group external-renderer rows by method and select the row with maximum mean_absolute_shift. Do not simply take the first row.

Numerical inputs remain competitive

The numerical HistGB model achieves ensemble MAE of 0.345, compared with the original-chart CNN's 0.355. It uses 111 numerical features from the same 20-day history and has no chart renderer in its input path.

Paper: Table 1. Source: ensemble_metrics.csv. This study does not show that image models outperform numerical models.

Scope of the evidence

The main test comprises 37 ETFs on 28 dates in January–July 2026, with three training seeds. Shared dates, correlated assets, and overlapping input histories limit independence. Date-cluster bootstrap intervals preserve same-date groups but do not establish independence across dates. The historical holdout is not prospective live forecasting or an external preregistration. External-renderer and 100-stock checks are later extensions.

These results do not establish profitable trading, GPT/VLM behavior, or universal robustness across charting software. The original code also discloses that candle-width randomization collapses after pixel quantization; effective continuous variation is in price padding.

For attribution, use the paper's BibTeX and keep the metric, aggregation, and test scope with any quoted result.
